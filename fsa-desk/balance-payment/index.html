“What is auto debit / auto pay — is it worth it?”

You schedule an automatic monthly electronic debit from your checking or savings account through your loan servicer, and Direct Loans get an interest-rate reduction: the standard reduction is 0.25%. TEMPORARY UPLIFT, in effect on the page now: starting July 1, 2026 the reduction is 1% — for borrowers with Direct Loans disbursed on or after July 1, 2012 — available through June 30, 2028 while you stay enrolled in auto pay and in repayment. Borrowers not yet enrolled have until 11:59 p.m. Eastern time on December 31, 2026 to enroll through their servicer.

“you receive a 0.25% interest rate reduction on Direct Loans”— the site’s own words, source page

“Who do I pay? Who is my servicer? Where's my balance?”

Direct Loans and ED-owned FFEL loans are paid to your loan servicer; FFEL loans not owned by ED are paid to the lender that made them; Perkins Loans are paid to your school or its billing agency. Find your servicer by logging in and visiting your My Aid page / account dashboard (“View loan servicer details”). Your billing statement tells you how much to pay. The servicers the page currently lists for ED-owned loans: Edfinancial, MOHELA, Aidvantage, Nelnet, ECSI, the Default Resolution Group, and CRI.

“Find your servicer by logging in below and visiting your My Aid page”— the site’s own words, source page

“When do I have to start paying?”

Once you graduate, drop below half-time, or leave school, the loan goes into repayment — with a grace period first: six months for Direct Subsidized/Unsubsidized and FFEL loans, nine months for Perkins. Interest accrues during grace; it's added to your balance but will NOT be capitalized. PLUS loans go into repayment as soon as the loan is fully disbursed (graduate/professional PLUS borrowers get an automatic deferment while in school and for six months after). Warning from the page: consolidating during grace means you give up the remainder of your grace period.

“you have a six-month grace period before you are required to start making regular payments”— the site’s own words, source page

“Can I pay extra, or pay early?”

Yes — you can make payments before they're due or pay more than the amount due each month, and paying a little extra reduces the interest you pay and the total cost of the loan over time. One limit the page states: a prepayment will not count as a qualifying payment in any loan forgiveness program.

“You can make payments before they are due or pay more than the amount due each month”— the site’s own words, source page

“A company offered to handle my loans for a fee — should I?”

No. Your loan servicer will help you for FREE — the page's standing warning is never to pay an outside company for help with your federal student loans. Related reassurance: if your servicer changes, there is no change in the terms of your loans.

“Your loan servicer will help you for FREE”— the site’s own words, source page

“How late is ‘default’, and what happens?”

Default occurs when you're at least nine months past due. The consequences the page names: damage to your credit rating and future borrowing ability, garnishment of your wages, and withholding of your tax refunds. Default itself is its own department in this aid — not yet built; the Default Resolution Group line is 1-800-621-3115.

“This occurs when you are at least nine months past due on your student loan”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Where's the Loan Simulator / my balance page?”

THE SITE DOESN’T SAY — gap on the source page

Two honest notes from these pages: they name a “Repayment Calculator” — not a “Loan Simulator” — for an early look at which plans you may be eligible for; and they state no on-site “view your balance” route beyond your billing statement and your servicer (My Aid shows your servicer details after login). Auto pay's accepted payment methods are not detailed on the page.

“you can use our Repayment Calculator to get an early look at which plans you may be eligible for”— the site’s own words, source page
