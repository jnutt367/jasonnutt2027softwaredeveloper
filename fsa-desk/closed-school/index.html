Closed School Discharge

Your school closed while you were enrolled — or within 180 days of leaving

Official resources

  • Closed School Discharge
  • Forgiveness, Cancellation & Discharge hub

Applications go to your loan servicer — find them via My Aid or 1-800-433-3243

“My school closed — how do I apply for the discharge? (all methods, one card)”

Every route ends at your LOAN SERVICER — all completed applications are sent there. ① Your loan holder will send you an application if you appear to meet the requirements: complete it and return it to your servicer. ② Or complete the PDF Closed School Loan Discharge Application yourself and return it to your servicer. ③ Or contact your servicer directly about the application process. There is NO deadline to apply. ⚠ Keep making payments while the application is being processed — the page says so plainly. And if your school closed on or after July 1, 2023, you may not need to apply at all (see the automatic-discharge card).

  • Don't know your servicer? Log in and check your My Aid page, or call 1-800-433-3243.
“Be sure to continue to make payments on your loan while your discharge application is being processed.”— the site’s own words, source page

“Who qualifies for a closed school discharge?”

You were unable to complete your program of study because your school closed, AND one of these is true: you were enrolled when the school closed · you were on an approved leave of absence when it closed · the school closed within 180 days after you withdrew. It covers Direct Loans, FFEL Program loans, and Perkins Loans received to attend that school — and the discharge is full.

  • A diploma or certificate for a lesser credential does NOT disqualify you if you did not complete your program of study.
“you were enrolled when your school closed”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Will my closed school discharge happen automatically?”

The site's own pages state it two ways — quote the one matching the caller's page; don't blend them. MAIN CLOSED-SCHOOL PAGE: if your school closes on or after July 1, 2023 and you meet the eligibility requirements, you will generally receive an automatic discharge one year after the date ED establishes as the school's official closure date — ED initiates it and your loan servicer notifies you — and you can apply sooner, as soon as the official closure date is confirmed. ‘HAS YOUR SCHOOL CLOSED?’ PAGE: for closures on or after Nov. 1, 2013, automatic discharge also requires that you have not enrolled at another school in the federal student aid programs within three years of the closure. Schools closed before July 1, 2023: not automatic under the main page — but you can still apply by paper application regardless of the date your school closed.

  • Denied in the past? You can submit a NEW application — it will be reviewed under the current eligibility requirements regardless of the date your school closed.
“you will generally receive an automatic closed school discharge one year after”— the site’s own words, source page

“My school closed — why wouldn't I qualify?”

Four disqualifiers: you graduated or completed your program at the closed school · you withdrew more than 180 days before the school closed (unless you can demonstrate an exceptional circumstance) · you're completing or have graduated from an approved teach-out agreement at another school · you're completing or have graduated from a program at a different branch or location of the school that closed. Two things that do NOT disqualify: transferring your credits to a completely different school, and refusing a teach-out (see the teach-out card).

“You withdrew more than 180 days before the school closed, unless you can demonstrate an exceptional circumstance.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“The school offered me a teach-out or transfer — do I have to take it?”

No — you have the right to refuse any teach-out agreement or transfer option the closing school suggests. A teach-out agreement is a written agreement between two institutions, approved by the school's accrediting agency and, if applicable, its state authorizing agency. The catch: if you ACCEPT a teach-out, you're ineligible for a closed school discharge — unless you do not complete your program while in the teach-out. A transfer to another branch or location of the SAME school works the same way; a transfer to a completely different school does not disqualify you.

“you have the right to refuse any teach-out agreement or transfer option”— the site’s own words, source page

“What does a closed school discharge actually do for me?”

No further obligation to repay the loan · reimbursement of payments made, voluntarily or through forced collection · the record of the loan and all repayment history, including any adverse history, deleted from your credit report · the discharged loan no longer counts against your annual and aggregate loan limits · if the closure kept you from finishing, the portion of your lifetime Pell Grant limit used at the closed school is restored, automatically, if you are eligible · and for federal taxes the discharged amount is not gross income — it does not need to be reported on a federal return.

“you will receive reimbursement of payments made voluntarily or through forced collection”— the site’s own words, source page

“I'm a parent — my child's school closed. Does this cover my PLUS loan?”

The ‘Has Your School Closed?’ page answers parents directly: as a parent, you may be eligible for a closed school loan discharge if the school closed before your child completed the program — contact your loan servicer for more information. (The loan lists on these pages name Direct, FFEL, and Perkins loans.)

“you may be eligible for a closed school loan discharge if the school closed before your child completed the program”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How do I get my transcripts and records from a closed school?”

Obtain your academic and financial aid records — you may need them to attend another school or for the discharge itself. Before closing, the school was required to make accommodations for you to access your academic records indefinitely. For transcripts, contact the state licensing agency in the state where the school was located to find out if the state has the records. ED provides all closed school records to loan servicers and guaranty agencies — and you can still be eligible for discharge even if ED did not prepare a fact sheet for your school.

“Contact the state licensing agency in the state where the school was located”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Which rules is ED using for closed school discharges right now?”

On Aug. 7, 2023, a federal court delayed the effective date of the latest regulations governing borrower defense and closed school discharges. Borrowers may still apply for a closed school discharge, and ED will process applications using earlier rules; ED will not process any application under the 2023 rule unless and until the effective date is reinstated.

“we will process applications using earlier rules”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Does a closed school discharge cover my private student loans?”

No. The page's information pertains only to federal student loans. For a private loan, the borrower contacts the private lender that made the loan to discuss options.

“Information provided on this page pertains only to your federal student loans.”— the site’s own words, source page

The closed school pages never mention TEACH Grants — the site doesn't say. (TEACH does appear in this aid where the official pages address it: in the TPD area, where a TPD discharge also relieves the TEACH Grant service obligation.)
