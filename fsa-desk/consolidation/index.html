Loan Consolidation

Combining federal loans into one Direct Consolidation Loan — free, and irreversible

Official resources

  • Direct Consolidation Loans
  • Consolidation application

Before/while applying: 1-800-433-3243 · After applying: your new consolidation servicer

“How do I consolidate — all my options?”

★ EASIEST: online at StudentAid.gov/loan-consolidation — most people finish in under 30 minutes, and you can save a draft and complete it later. Or by U.S. mail: the application package goes to the consolidation servicer of your choice. There is NO application fee — consolidation is at no cost to you. While it processes, keep making payments on the old loans until your consolidation servicer tells you they've been paid off (unless you're in deferment, forbearance, or grace). Repayment on the new loan begins within 60 days after it's disbursed; Aidvantage processes all Direct Consolidation Loan applications, so mail from them is expected.

“There is no application fee to consolidate your federal education loans into a Direct Consolidation Loan”— the site’s own words, source page

“I consolidated after July 1, 2026 — why are my plan choices so limited?”

The page's rule: consolidate on or after July 1, 2026 and you can enroll in only the Tiered Standard Plan and RAP — for ALL your Direct Loans, including ones received before July 1, 2026. Carved out, and permitted to be repaid separately under Tiered Standard: parent Direct PLUS loans, Direct Consolidation Loans that paid off a parent PLUS loan, and the double-consolidation chain. If the new consolidation INCLUDES parent PLUS loans, the warning is sharper: only Tiered Standard access for those loans — and Tiered Standard isn't eligible for PSLF. Related standing warning: don't include both parent PLUS loans and loans for a dependent's education in the same consolidation, to maximize plan options.

“you'll be able to enroll in only the Tiered Standard Plan and the Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP) for all of your Direct Loans”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can I undo a consolidation?”

No. Once loans are combined into a Direct Consolidation Loan, the consolidation can't be reversed — the loans that were consolidated are considered paid off and no longer exist.

“Once your loans are combined into a Direct Consolidation Loan, the consolidation can't be reversed”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What is a Direct Consolidation Loan?”

It combines one or more federal education loans into a single new Direct Consolidation Loan — for the purpose of lowering your monthly payment or gaining access to federal forgiveness programs. Afterward you have one loan servicer and one monthly payment, and you can reach up to 30 years to repay. Consolidating is also the only way to switch servicers.

“consolidate (combine) one or more federal education loans into a new Direct Consolidation Loan”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Which of my loans can I consolidate?”

Most federal student loans — Direct and FFEL included. Private education loans are NOT eligible. Generally you're eligible after you graduate, leave school, or drop below half-time, and the loans must be in repayment or in a grace period. You don't have to consolidate all of your eligible loans. Generally you can't consolidate an existing consolidation loan by itself — you must add another eligible loan (narrow exceptions exist for a single FFEL Consolidation Loan: default-aversion with an IDR agreement, qualifying for PSLF, or using the active-duty no-interest-accrual benefit).

“Most federal student loans—including Direct Loans and FFEL Program Loans—are eligible for consolidation”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What interest rate will the new loan have?”

A fixed rate: the weighted average of the interest rates on the loans being consolidated, rounded UP to the nearest one-eighth of one percent.

“weighted average of the interest rates on the loans being consolidated, rounded up to the nearest one-eighth of one percent”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What happens to the interest I already owe when I consolidate?”

Any outstanding interest becomes part of the new loan's original principal balance — so interest may accrue on a higher principal than if you hadn't consolidated. Stretching the term lowers the payment but results in more payments and more interest overall.

“any outstanding interest on the loans you consolidate becomes part of the original principal balance on your consolidation loan”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Could I lose anything by consolidating?”

Yes — you may lose certain borrower benefits, such as interest rate discounts, principal rebates, or some loan cancellation benefits. Perkins caution the page states: if you're employed in an occupation that would qualify you for Perkins Loan cancellation benefits, you might not want to include your Perkins Loans.

“you may lose certain borrower benefits—such as interest rate discounts, principal rebates, or some loan cancellation benefits”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can I consolidate a defaulted loan?”

Yes, with a condition: either make satisfactory repayment arrangements first — defined as three consecutive monthly payments — or agree to repay the new Direct Consolidation Loan under an income-driven plan. If you're consolidating a defaulted loan, you MUST select an IDR plan. A loan under wage garnishment or judgment can't be consolidated until the garnishment is lifted or the judgment vacated.

“make satisfactory repayment arrangements (defined as three consecutive monthly payments) on the loan before you consolidate”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Will consolidating mess up my PSLF count or buyback?”

THE SITE DOESN’T SAY — gap on the source page

The application page carries one caution verbatim: “Consolidating could affect your eligibility to buy back certain months.” Beyond that, THE CONSOLIDATION PAGES DON'T SAY whether consolidation resets IDR or PSLF payment counts — the weighted-average credit rule for PSLF lives on the PSLF page and is carded in this aid's PSLF area; don't blend the two at the desk. The pages also state no processing timeline.

“Consolidating could affect your eligibility to buy back certain months”— the site’s own words, source page
