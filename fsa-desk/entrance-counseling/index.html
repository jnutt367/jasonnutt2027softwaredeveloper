Entrance Counseling

Required before a first Direct Loan can be paid out — about 30 minutes, in one sitting

Official resources

  • Entrance Counseling — log in or view the demo
  • Master Promissory Note
  • Annual Student Loan Acknowledgment

Federal Student Aid help: 1-800-433-3243

“What are all my options for completing entrance counseling?”

★ Easiest: log in and complete it online — pick your door on the Entrance Counseling page: “I am an Undergraduate Student” or “I am a Graduate or Professional Student.” Each door also offers a View Demo so you can see it before starting. The page states no paper or phone route.

“Students receiving the following types of loans for the first time: Direct Subsidized Loans; Direct Unsubsidized Loans; Direct PLUS Loans for graduate or professional students who qualify for the limited exception.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Do I have to do entrance counseling?”

Yes — if this is your first Direct Subsidized or Direct Unsubsidized Loan. Graduate and professional students: it's also required if you haven't previously received a Direct Unsubsidized Loan, or a Direct PLUS Loan under the limited exception.

“Entrance counseling for undergraduate students is required if you have not previously received a Direct Subsidized Loan or a Direct Unsubsidized Loan.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How long does entrance counseling take?”

About 30 minutes — and it has to be finished in a single session.

“Entrance Counseling takes about 30 minutes to complete and it must be completed in a single session.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What is entrance counseling, exactly?”

A required walkthrough of your loan's terms and your rights and responsibilities — what a loan is, how interest works, repayment options, and how to avoid delinquency and default.

“Entrance counseling ensures you understand the terms and conditions of your loan and your rights and responsibilities.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I stepped away and it logged me out — what happened?”

Sessions time out after 15 minutes of inactivity — the page warns about this up front. Plan to finish in one sitting.

“Sessions time out after 15 minutes of inactivity.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What do I need in front of me before I start?”

Your verified StudentAid.gov account, your school name(s), your Financial Aid Offer letter, and a breakdown of your school's tuition and fees.

“Financial Aid Offer (this is the letter from your school that outlines your loans, scholarships, and other aid if offered)”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I finished — when do I get my loan money?”

A record of your completion is sent to the schools you selected; once the school has it, you can receive your loan money.

“a record of your completion will be sent to the schools you selected, and you can then receive your loan money.”— the site’s own words, source page
