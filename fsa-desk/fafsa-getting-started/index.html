FAFSA — Getting Started

Starting the form, the 2027–28 dates, contributors, and the paper route

FAFSA help (FSAIC): 1-800-433-3243

“What are all my options for completing the FAFSA?”

★ Easiest: online at StudentAid.gov — select “Start New Form.” Paper route: download and print the FAFSA PDF (Resources page, English or Spanish) and mail it to the address printed on the form. Free help while you fill: the in-form question-mark icon, the Aidan® virtual assistant, and the FAFSA Help articles. The 1-800 line helps with the form and process — the site states no way to file by phone.

“If you can’t fill out the online FAFSA form, you can fill out a FAFSA PDF.”— the site’s own words, source page

“What's the federal deadline for the 2027–28 FAFSA?”

11:59 p.m. Central time on June 30, 2028. (Corrections run later — by Sept. 12, 2028; see the Corrections area.)

  • For the earlier 2026–27 form: submit by June 30, 2027; corrections by Sept. 12, 2027.
“The FAFSA form must be submitted by 11:59 p.m. Central time (CT) on June 30, 2028.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Do I need an account to start the FAFSA?”

Yes. You need a StudentAid.gov account to start, access, or submit the form — and every required contributor (parent or spouse) needs their own account too.

“You need a StudentAid.gov account to start, access, or submit your FAFSA form.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Who counts as a contributor on the FAFSA?”

Anyone required to provide information on the form, sign it, and consent to the IRS tax transfer: you, your spouse, a biological or adoptive parent, or a parent's spouse. Contributors are invited from inside the form — the pages read for this aid don't walk the invite steps; the hub's “Accept an Invitation” button is where an invited contributor lands.

“A contributor is anyone (you, your spouse, your biological or adoptive parent, or your parent’s spouse)”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Who should fill out the FAFSA?”

Any student, regardless of income, who wants to be considered for federal, state, or school aid — grants, scholarships, work-study, and loans. Schools use the form to determine how much aid you're eligible to receive.

“Any student, regardless of income, who wants to be considered for federal, state, and school financial aid programs.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How long does the FAFSA take?”

Most people finish their own section in 12 minutes or less.

“People typically finish their section of the FAFSA form in 12 minutes or less.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How do I start the online FAFSA?”

Select “Start New Form,” pick the year (2027–28 or 2026–27), log in with your account username and password, and select “Student.” A parent starting a dependent child's form selects “Parent.”

“Log in using your StudentAid.gov account username and password.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What do I need before I start?”

A verified account username and password (your FSA ID), your parent or spouse contributor's email address, and income and asset information if required.

“Verified account username and password (FSA ID)”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What documents might I need to have ready?”

You can usually start — and finish — without any documents, since most people import tax info directly. Records you might need: tax returns; records of child support received; current savings and checking balances; cash over $1,000; and the value and debt owed on non-retirement investments, businesses, and farms.

  • You can start, and usually finish, your FAFSA form without referring to any documents.
“Current amount of cash if it’s more than $1,000”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Should I mail my tax documents in with my FAFSA?”

No. Keep your records — do not mail them.

“Keep these records! Do not mail them to us.”— the site’s own words, source page

“Is the FAFSA really free?”

Yes — there is no fee. If a website asks you to pay to fill out the FAFSA form, you're on the wrong website.

“If a website asks you to pay to fill out the FAFSA form, you’re on the wrong website.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Which tax year does the 2027–28 FAFSA use?”

2025 tax information. (The 2026–27 form asks for 2024 tax information.)

“The 2027–28 FAFSA form asks for 2025 tax information.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Does my state have its own FAFSA deadline?”

Yes — every state sets its own. Check the deadlines page's state table, and your state higher education agency's site for the latest.

  • Missing a state deadline does not affect your federal aid eligibility — the hub says file anyway.
“Visit your state higher education agency’s website for the most up-to-date state deadline.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Do colleges set their own FAFSA deadlines?”

Yes — each college or career/trade school may have its own deadline. Check with the school.

“Each college and career/trade school may have its own deadline.”— the site’s own words, source page

“If you can’t fill out the online FAFSA form, you can fill out a FAFSA PDF.”— the site’s own words, source page

“What if my parent or spouse won't consent to the IRS tax transfer?”

Then the student won't be eligible for federal student aid — even if the contributor types the tax information in manually. Consent isn't optional for eligibility.

“the student won’t be eligible for federal student aid.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“My parents are divorced — which one goes on my FAFSA?”

The parent who provided more financial support during the last 12 months. If support was equal — or neither parent supports you — it's the parent with the greater income and assets.

“the parent who provided more financial support during the last 12 months is the contributor”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“My parents are married — do both have to fill out a section?”

If they filed taxes jointly, only one parent is a contributor and reports for both. If they didn't file jointly, both parents are contributors.

“If your parents filed taxes jointly, only one parent is required to be a contributor”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can my parent do their part if they don't have a Social Security number?”

Yes — a parent without an SSN can create a StudentAid.gov account and contribute. Don't enter an ITIN in the SSN field.

“Parents who don’t have a Social Security number (SSN) are able to create a StudentAid.gov account”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What if I can't contact my parents — abuse, incarceration, abandonment?”

Answer “Yes” to the unusual-circumstances question. You'll be considered provisionally independent: you can skip the parent questions and submit without a parent signature — then follow up with your school's financial aid office, which decides what documentation is needed.

“You will be able to skip questions about your parents on the FAFSA form and submit it without a parent signature.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“My parents refuse to give their information — does that make me independent?”

No — refusal alone doesn't make you independent. You can still submit without parent information, but you won't receive a Student Aid Index (SAI), and you may qualify for a Direct Unsubsidized Loan only.

“If you submit your FAFSA form without parent information, you will not receive a Student Aid Index (SAI).”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Do my parents' citizenship or immigration status affect my aid?”

No. Your parents' citizenship status does not affect your eligibility for federal student aid — and the FAFSA doesn't ask about their status.

“Your parents’ citizenship status does not affect your eligibility for federal student aid.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What does a parent have to provide on the FAFSA?”

Basic identity and living-situation information and their financial circumstances — plus consent and approval for their federal tax information to transfer directly from the IRS into the form.

“They’ll also have to provide consent and approval to have their federal tax information transferred directly from the IRS into the FAFSA form.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I'm having trouble with my account — can you help my parent sort it out?”

The contact center can discuss FSA ID (account) issues only with the account holder — not with a parent, spouse, or anyone else on their behalf. The holder has to call about their own account.

“The contact center can discuss FSA ID issues only with the account holder.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I filed my FAFSA with the wrong Social Security number — what do I do?”

Update your information in Account Settings; once the Social Security Administration verifies your account status, correct the FAFSA form itself. If you're sure the SSN is correct, call SSA at 1-800-772-1213, then call the FSAIC at 1-800-433-3243 to manually sync your account with SSA.

“Once the Social Security Administration (SSA) verifies your account status, update the information on your FAFSA form”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What don't these pages tell us about getting started?”

THE SITE DOESN’T SAY — gap on the source page

THE SITE DOESN'T SAY: the contributor-invite steps aren't walked on these pages (they live inside the form); no paper-form processing time is stated here (the Corrections area carries the site's one timing: status roughly 7–10 days after mailing); and no wait time is published for SSA verification of an account. When the page offers no answer, this aid invents none.
