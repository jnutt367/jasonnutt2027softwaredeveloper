FAFSA form

The application for federal student aid — grants, loans, and work-study.

  • Where it lives: Online at StudentAid.gov — select “Start New Form” (the easiest route). Paper route: the FAFSA PDF on the Resources page, mailed to the address printed on the form.
  • Who sends it: The student submits it; each required contributor (parent or spouse) completes their own part.

PSLF form (Public Service Loan Forgiveness Certification & Application)

Certifies qualifying employment — and, once 120 qualifying payments are made, applies for forgiveness.

  • Where it lives: Built easiest in the PSLF Help Tool, which can request the employer’s electronic signature. A signed paper form goes three ways: upload it via My Activity after logging in · mail to U.S. Department of Education, P.O. Box 300010, Greenville, TX 75403 · fax to 540-212-2415. There is no email route.
  • Who sends it: The borrower completes it; the employer certifies the employment and signs.

PSLF Reconsideration request

Asks FSA to re-check a PSLF payment count or determination.

  • Where it lives: Online, on the reconsideration page — “Submit a reconsideration request,” logged in. Supporting documents can be uploaded with the request but are not required.
  • Who sends it: The borrower. (PSLF Buyback is requested through this same door — choose “PSLF Buyback” as the reconsideration type, and only with 120 months of qualifying employment already certified.)

Borrower Defense application

Applies for discharge of the loans tied to a school that misled the borrower.

  • Where it lives: The online application tool (easiest), or the PDF application (OMB No. 1845-0163). Honest gap: the page states no address for mailing a paper application — confirm it from the form itself or the borrower defense hotline, 1-855-279-6207, before mailing.
  • Who sends it: The borrower.

TPD Discharge application

Applies for discharge of federal loans (and a TEACH Grant service obligation) due to total and permanent disability.

  • Where it lives: The online “Apply for TPD Discharge” tool, or the paper application. Its doors are ED’s, not the servicer’s: upload via My Activity after logging in · mail to Greenville, TX 75403 · fax 540-212-2415.
  • Who sends it: The borrower (or their representative); eligibility is proved by a medical professional’s certification or by VA or SSA documentation.

Closed School Loan Discharge Application

Applies for discharge when your school closed while you were enrolled, or soon after you withdrew.

  • Where it lives: Your loan holder sends you an application if you appear to meet the requirements, or complete the PDF yourself — every route ends at your loan servicer. There is no deadline to apply.
  • Who sends it: The borrower, sent to their loan servicer.

Teacher Loan Forgiveness Application

Applies for forgiveness (up to $17,500) after five consecutive years of qualifying teaching service.

  • Where it lives: Submitted to your loan servicer — a separate form to each servicer if your loans sit with more than one.
  • Who sends it: The borrower submits it; the chief administrative officer of the school or educational service agency completes the certification section.
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

False Certification discharge applications (four)

One application per circumstance: High School Graduation Status · Disqualifying Status · Identity Theft · Unauthorized Signature/Payment.

  • Where it lives: Complete the application that applies to your circumstance; for more information, contact your loan servicer.
  • Who sends it: The borrower whose circumstance matches. (A separate Group application exists for state attorneys general and nonprofit legal-services representatives filing for a group.)

Direct Consolidation Loan application

Combines federal education loans into one new Direct Consolidation Loan. There is no application fee.

  • Where it lives: Online at StudentAid.gov/loan-consolidation (easiest — most people finish in under 30 minutes), or the paper package by mail to the consolidation servicer you choose.
  • Who sends it: The borrower. (Aidvantage processes all Direct Consolidation Loan applications.)
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

The desk card that walks through it — Loan Consolidation →

Income-Driven Repayment Plan Request

Applies for an income-driven repayment plan — and later recertifies it each year.

  • Where it lives: Online at StudentAid.gov/idr (easiest — most people finish in 10 minutes or less), or a paper form. If your loans sit with more than one servicer, submit a separate request to each servicer.
  • Who sends it: The borrower.
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

The desk card that walks through it — Income-Driven Repayment →

Master Promissory Note (MPN)

Your legal promise to repay the loan(s) and any accrued interest and fees to the U.S. Department of Education.

  • Where it lives: Online on the MPN page — three login doors: undergraduate · graduate or professional student · parent of an undergraduate student. The page states no paper route.
  • Who sends it: The borrower for that loan type signs; complete an MPN for each loan type you intend to receive.
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

Direct PLUS Loan application

Applies for a PLUS loan — and sets the amount, how the school may use the funds, and who receives a credit balance.

  • Where it lives: Online on the PLUS Loan Application page — pick “I am a Parent of a Student” or “I am a Graduate or Professional Student.”
  • Who sends it: The parent borrower or the graduate/professional student borrower.
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

The desk card that walks through it — PLUS Application and Credit Counseling →

Endorser Addendum

Lets an endorser with acceptable credit back a PLUS borrower who did not pass the credit check.

  • Where it lives: Online on the Endorse a PLUS Loan page — open it with the borrower’s last name and either the endorser code or the award identification number of the loan.
  • Who sends it: The endorser.

PLUS credit appeal (documentation of extenuating circumstances)

Asks FSA to reconsider a PLUS denial based on adverse credit history.

  • Where it lives: Filed online from your account. If you can’t file online, reach out to the contact center at 1-800-433-3243.
  • Who sends it: The denied PLUS applicant. (An approved appeal plus PLUS Credit Counseling lets the school say whether a loan is available.)

TEACH Grant Agreement to Serve or Repay

Your signed agreement to complete the four-year teaching service obligation — or repay the grant as a Direct Unsubsidized Loan.

  • Where it lives: Online on the TEACH Grant page, every year you receive the grant — after TEACH Grant Initial and Subsequent Counseling, in that order.
  • Who sends it: The student receiving the TEACH Grant.
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

The desk card that walks through it — TEACH Grants →

TEACH Certification of Teaching Service

Documents each completed year of qualifying teaching service under the agreement.

  • Where it lives: Online at StudentAid.gov/teach-certification, or by mail or fax: Federal Student Aid Programs - TEACH, P.O. Box 300010, Greenville, TX 75403 · fax 540-212-2415.
  • Who sends it: The teacher submits it; the chief administrative officer confirms the service.
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

Loan rehabilitation agreement

Sets the payment plan (9 payments over 9–10 consecutive months) that brings a defaulted loan current and removes the default record.

  • Where it lives: There is no public form to download — the agreement is made with the Default Resolution Group: 1-800-621-3115 · TTY 1-877-825-9923. Rehabilitation paperwork fax: 1-240-931-3323.
  • Who sends it: The borrower, together with the Default Resolution Group.
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

The desk card that walks through it — Getting Out of Default →

Death discharge (no application form)

Discharges federal loans due to the death of the borrower — or, for a parent PLUS loan, the death of the parent or of the student.

  • Where it lives: No application form is named. A family member or other representative provides the loan servicer acceptable documentation of the death: an original death certificate, a certified copy, or an accurate and complete photocopy of one of those.
  • Who sends it: A family member or other representative, to the loan servicer.
