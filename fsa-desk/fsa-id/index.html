FSA ID — Your StudentAid.gov Account

Creating the account, getting back in, locks and verification

Official resources

  • Creating your StudentAid.gov account
  • Help accessing your account

Account help: 1-800-433-3243

“How do I create my account (FSA ID)?”

★ An account is required to complete any process or form on StudentAid.gov. Create it from “Create Account” (site header or the sign-in page): it takes less than 10 minutes. You'll enter your name exactly as it appears on your Social Security card (if you have one), date of birth, an email address, and your SSN if you have one — and you'll set up two-step verification as part of creating it, because the account signs legal documents. SSA verification usually happens right away; if it shows “Pending,” that may take one to three days to resolve.

“Creating a StudentAid.gov account takes less than 10 minutes”— the site’s own words, source page

“I forgot my username — or which email I used.”

Use “Forgot email, phone, or username?” on the log-in page: provide your last name, date of birth, and SSN, verify your account, and choose an option to receive a secure code — it retrieves your email address, mobile number, or username. Shortcut worth knowing: you can log in with your email address or mobile phone number if you can't remember your username.

“Retrieve your email address, mobile phone number, or username by providing your personal information and verifying your account”— the site’s own words, source page

“My account is locked. Now what?”

Accounts lock after THREE unsuccessful log-in attempts. To unlock, use one of your verification methods — verified mobile phone or email, authenticator app, security key, or (if set up) challenge questions: provide the month and day of your birth, continue, enter the secure code, and you'll be prompted to create a new password.

“Your account is locked after three unsuccessful log-in attempts”— the site’s own words, source page

“Who in our family needs their own account?”

Each person for their own part. A parent or spouse who is a required FAFSA contributor must create their OWN account to contribute — including a contributor who doesn't have an SSN; the page says they must create an account to contribute to the student's form. Students must have an SSN to create an account, unless they're from the Freely Associated States (Micronesia, Marshall Islands, Palau).

“An account is required to complete any process or form on StudentAid.gov”— the site’s own words, source page

“Can my parent and I share one email for our accounts?”

No — the email address must be unique and can't be used with anyone else's StudentAid.gov account, including a parent's or spouse's. Your email also carries your two-step verification codes at log-in; you can add and verify a mobile number too, and choose to have codes texted to it.

“The email address you provide must be unique and can't be used with anyone else's StudentAid.gov account”— the site’s own words, source page

“I forgot my password.”

“Forgot Password?” on the log-in page: reset by providing your date of birth and verifying your account. Careful with guessing — multiple failed attempts could lock your account (next card).

“Reset your password by providing your date of birth and verifying your account”— the site’s own words, source page

“I can't get my two-step code — my phone is gone.”

Use your backup code: it was generated when you created your account or when you enabled two-step verification. It exists exactly for this — logged in but locked out of your two-step method.

“If you logged in but don't have access to your two-step verification method, use your backup code”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can my mom just use my account? Can I make a second one?”

No, on both. The account signs legally binding documents, so only you can use it — the site's own comparison: giving someone else access is like having another person forge your signature on a legal contract. And you can't simply create another account in the future; one person, one account.

“Your StudentAid.gov account allows you to sign legally binding documents. This means that only you can use it”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I don't have a Social Security number.”

On the create-account form, leave the SSN field blank and select “What if I don't have a Social Security number?” — that path exists on the form. (Student accounts are the exception to watch: a student must have an SSN unless from the Freely Associated States; contributors without an SSN still create accounts to contribute — see the who-needs card.)

“leave the SSN field blank and select “What if I don't have a Social Security number?””— the site’s own words, source page

“What are the password rules? What if I lost EVERYTHING — email, phone, backup code?”

THE SITE DOESN’T SAY — gap on the source page

THE PAGES DON'T SAY: no password-complexity rules are published on them, no unlock waiting period is stated, and no detailed recovery path is given for a caller who has lost their email, their phone, AND their backup code. That caller goes to a human: 1-800-433-3243. Don't improvise account-recovery steps the site doesn't publish.
