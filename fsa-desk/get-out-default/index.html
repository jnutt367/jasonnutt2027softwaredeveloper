Getting Out of Default

Three routes out — pay in full, rehabilitation, consolidation

Official resources

  • Getting Out of Default
  • Rehabilitation FAQs
  • Consolidation
  • Defaulted Loans Support Center

Default Resolution Group (defaulted loans): 1-800-621-3115 · TTY 1-877-825-9923

“What are ALL my options for getting out of default?”

The site states three routes: ① repay the loan in full, ② loan rehabilitation ★ — the route that removes the default record from your credit history, and ③ consolidation into a new Direct Consolidation Loan — the default record stays in your history on that route. Fresh Start is not a fourth route: it ended Oct. 2, 2024 (carded on this page).

  • Rehabilitation removes the default record from your credit history (the late payments stay); consolidation leaves the record in place.
  • Rehabilitation takes 9 payments over 9–10 consecutive months; consolidation needs an IDR agreement on the new loan, or 3 consecutive voluntary on-time full payments first.
“repay in full · rehabilitation · consolidation”— the site’s own words, source page

“How does loan rehabilitation work?”

You agree to make 9 voluntary, reasonable, and affordable payments, each within 20 days of its due date, over 9–10 consecutive months. Make the 9 and the loan is out of default.

  • Perkins loans differ: rehabilitation takes 9 consecutive full payments.
“9 voluntary, reasonable, affordable payments within 20 days of due date over 9–10 consecutive months”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How is my rehabilitation payment amount set?”

It's income-based: 10% or 15% of your annual discretionary income, divided by 12 — discretionary income being income above 150% of the poverty guideline. If that amount doesn't work, an alternative amount is available on request.

“10% or 15% of annual discretionary income ÷ 12”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can I still use Fresh Start to get out of default?”

No — Fresh Start ENDED on Oct. 2, 2024. The site mentions it only historically, in one FAQ article; there is no Fresh Start page and no Fresh Start door to send anyone to. The live routes out are the three on this page: pay in full, rehabilitation, consolidation.

Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What does rehabilitation do to my credit?”

Rehabilitation removes the default record from your credit history — that is its edge over consolidation. The late payments that led into default stay on the record.

“removes the default record from credit history”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can I rehabilitate the same loan twice?”

Not yet: rehabilitation is one-time per loan today. That changes July 1, 2027 — from that date, a loan may be rehabilitated twice.

“Rehab is ONE-TIME — twice per loan from July 1, 2027”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I'm being garnished — do those payments count toward my 9?”

No — garnishment and offset payments don't count toward the 9 rehabilitation payments, and they continue until you're no longer in default or you've made 5 rehab payments. (Withholding itself is currently paused — see the Collections area.)

“don't count toward the 9”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How do I consolidate out of default?”

Two doors into a new Direct Consolidation Loan: agree to repay the new loan under an income-driven repayment (IDR) plan, or first make 3 consecutive, voluntary, on-time, full payments on the defaulted loan.

“agree to IDR on the new loan OR make 3 consecutive voluntary on-time full payments first”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“There's a court order or judgment on my default — can I still consolidate?”

A garnishment/court-order restriction stands in the way first: the order must be lifted, or the judgment vacated, before consolidation can go through.

“order must be lifted / judgment vacated”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Anything special about consolidating a defaulted Consolidation Loan?”

Yes: a defaulted Direct Consolidation Loan needs another eligible loan included in the new consolidation. A defaulted FFEL Consolidation Loan has its own door — IDR-only.

“Defaulted Direct Consolidation needs another eligible loan included”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Which IDR plan will my new consolidation loan be on?”

From July 1, 2026, the only IDR plan available for new Direct Consolidation Loans is the Repayment Assistance Plan.

“the only IDR plan for new Direct Consolidation Loans = Repayment Assistance Plan”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Rehabilitation or consolidation — which should I pick?”

The trade the site lays out: rehabilitation removes the default record from your credit history but takes 9 payments over 9–10 months, and it's one-time per loan (twice from July 1, 2027); consolidation can move faster — IDR agreement or 3 full payments — but the default record stays in your history.

“consolidation leaves it”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What if I just pay the whole loan off?”

Repaying in full is the first of the three routes the site names, and it ends the default outright. Loan payments for defaulted loans are sent to the National Payment Center, P.O. Box 790336, St. Louis, MO 63179-0336; DRG is 1-800-621-3115 (TTY 1-877-825-9923).

Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Where do I send rehabilitation paperwork?”

The rehabilitation fax printed for this process: 1-240-931-3323. For the agreement itself, the Default Resolution Group is the door: 1-800-621-3115 · TTY 1-877-825-9923.
