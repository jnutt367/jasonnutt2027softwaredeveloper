Income-Driven Repayment

RAP · IBR · ICR · PAYE — payments based on income and family size

Official resources

  • Income-Driven Repayment Plans
  • Apply for IDR / recertify

IDR application help: 1-800-433-3243 · Plan itself runs through your loan servicer

“How do I apply for an income-driven plan — all my options?”

★ EASIEST: apply online at StudentAid.gov/idr — the page says most people complete it in 10 minutes or less, and you can save and finish later. You can also submit a paper form. Either way, if your loans sit with more than one servicer, you must submit a separate request to EACH servicer. You'll need a verified FSA ID, financial and personal information, and your spouse's information if applicable. The same /idr page is also where you recertify.

“You can apply for an IDR plan online or submit a paper form”— the site’s own words, source page

“What is recertification? Do I really have to do it every year?”

Yes — you must recertify your income and family size every year you're on an IDR plan, even if nothing changed, by giving your servicer updated information. If you consented to federal tax-data access and are eligible for auto-recertification, FSA recertifies you automatically on the date. If your income or family size changes mid-year, you can recertify immediately and/or ask for a recalculation.

“You must recertify your plan even if there has been no change in your income or family size”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I missed my recertification — what happens now?”

Your payment stops being based on income and family size, so it may increase: the new amount is the 10-year Standard amount figured on the balance you owed when you entered repayment (RAP) or the plan (PAYE/ICR). On IBR, unpaid interest capitalizes — it's added to principal, and you then pay interest on the increased balance. Getting back: give your servicer updated income information (for PAYE, your updated income must be low enough to qualify again).

“your monthly payment amount won't be based on your income and family size, which means it may increase”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What is an income-driven plan, in one line?”

Your monthly payment is based on your income and family size (or number of dependents) — and it can be as low as $0 per month. The IDR hub also says plainly: if you're seeking PSLF, you should repay under an IDR plan.

“bases your monthly student loan payment amount on your income and family size or number of dependents”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Which IDR plans are there, and what does each charge?”

RAP — 1% to 10% of adjusted gross income, divided by 12, minus $50 per dependent; balance discharged after 30 years. IBR — 10% of discretionary income and 20 years if you first borrowed after July 1, 2014 (and before July 1, 2026); 15% and 25 years if you borrowed before July 1, 2014. ICR — the lesser of 20% of discretionary income or a 12-year fixed payment adjusted for income; 25 years. PAYE — 10% of discretionary income; 20 years. Caps: IBR and PAYE payments are never more than the 10-year Standard amount; ICR and RAP payments are NOT capped.

“any remaining loan balance may be discharged after you've made the required number of qualifying monthly payments”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can I get on PAYE?”

PAYE needs a new borrower on or after October 1, 2007 who also received a Direct Loan disbursement on or after October 1, 2011 — and your PAYE payment must be less than the 10-year Standard payment. Remember the July 1, 2026 line from the plans hub: a first disbursement on or after that date loses IBR/ICR/PAYE eligibility (RAP instead).

“a new borrower on or after Oct. 1, 2007”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I'm a parent PLUS borrower — which IDR plans can I use?”

Parent Direct PLUS loans are not eligible under RAP, PAYE, or IBR. The one door is ICR, and only if the PLUS loan is consolidated — a Direct Consolidation Loan that repaid a parent PLUS loan is eligible under ICR only. (The PSLF area of this aid carries the matching caution: consolidations containing parent PLUS loans are ineligible for RAP.) Perkins loans become eligible for all four plans if consolidated; FFEL loans are eligible under IBR, and under the others if consolidated.

“Eligible if consolidated”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can I get on IDR to fix my defaulted loans?”

Not directly — defaulted loans are not eligible for repayment under IDR plans. (Consolidation of a defaulted loan requires selecting an IDR plan for the NEW loan — see this aid's Consolidation area; default itself is its own department, not yet built.)

“Defaulted loans are not eligible for repayment under IDR plans”— the site’s own words, source page

“What's the catch with IDR?”

Two the page states: you'll likely pay more in interest over time — sometimes significantly more — and under current IRS rules you might owe income tax on any amount discharged at the end. The forgiveness horizons themselves: 20, 25, or 30 years of qualifying payments depending on the plan.

“you will likely pay more in interest over time—sometimes significantly more”— the site’s own words, source page

“What about the SAVE plan?”

THE SITE DOESN’T SAY — gap on the source page

THE SITE DOESN'T SAY — on the pages this area is built from. The SAVE plan is not named anywhere on the IDR hub or the IDR application page (both read in full, October 22, 2026): no status, no terms, no route. Don't quote a SAVE status at the desk from these pages; the caller's servicer and the site's announcements govern. Gaps on the same pages: the RAP 1%–10% income-tier brackets and the definition of “discretionary income” are not stated.
