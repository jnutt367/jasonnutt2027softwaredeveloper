New here? What this is — and how a card works

Built by an agent, for agents. Every card in here answers a caller's question in StudentAid.gov's own words — with the page it came from, so you can check it yourself. It's a personal, unofficial job aid; the site is the truth.

How a card works: ① the caller's question, in their words · ② the one-line answer · ③ the site's own words, quoted short · ④ the link to the source page. When a card is marked “the site doesn't say,” the source page offers no answer — so none is invented.

Departments match the functional-area board — open your department, then your area. The search above crosses every card in every department.

Built for the team that answers — every card in here serves somebody on the other end of the line.

One real card — the pattern, start to finish

Every card in this aid is built exactly like this one. It lives on the Outages & Troubleshooting page:

“The site won't let me log in — is it down?”

Check the clock first: StudentAid.gov has regularly scheduled maintenance on Sundays from 3 a.m. to 11 a.m. Eastern time. During that window you can view website pages, but you cannot create an account or log in. Most “the site is down” calls on a Sunday morning are this window, not an outage.

Contacts & forms

General Federal Student Aid help — the main 1-800 line (FAFSA questions, account access, finding your servicer, anything not tied to one program): 1-800-433-3243 (1-800-4-FED-AID).

The special lines ring the program teams directly: Discharge & forgiveness support (PSLF form help, TPD, TEACH): 1-888-303-7818 · Borrower Defense hotline: 1-855-279-6207 · Defaulted loans (Default Resolution Group): 1-800-621-3115. Payments, billing, and plan changes go to the borrower's loan servicer.

PSLF paper form — mail: U.S. Department of Education, P.O. Box 300010, Greenville, TX 75403 · fax: 540-212-2415.
Borrower Defense mail: U.S. Department of Education, Federal Student Aid Information Center, P.O. Box 1854, Monticello, KY 42633.

How this reference stays true

Every card is drawn from the official StudentAid.gov page named on it, verified on the date shown. When the site changes, the cards are re-checked and swapped — this address never moves. Cards marked “the site doesn’t say” are honest gaps: the source page offers no answer, so none is invented here.
