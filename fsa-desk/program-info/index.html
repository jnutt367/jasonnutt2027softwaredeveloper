“What grants are there, and how much?”

The headline figures the grants page states: PELL — up to $7,395 for the 2026–27 award year, for undergraduates with exceptional financial need who haven't earned a bachelor's, graduate, or professional degree. FSEOG — up to $4,000 a year, undergraduates with exceptional financial need. TEACH — up to $4,000 a year, for students in programs preparing them to teach in a high-need field (its obligation is carded in depth in the Loan Management department's TEACH area).

“Up to $7,395 for the 2026–27 award year”— the site’s own words, source page

“What loans are there, and how much can I borrow?”

DIRECT SUBSIDIZED — up to $5,500 depending on grade level and dependency status, for eligible undergraduates with demonstrated financial need. DIRECT UNSUBSIDIZED — undergrad amounts run by grade level and dependency status (the page states no single flat figure); graduate up to $20,500; professional students up to $50,000. DIRECT PLUS (grad/professional) — maximum is the cost of attendance minus any other financial aid. PARENT PLUS — for parents of dependent undergraduates: cost of attendance minus other aid where the parent qualifies; where they don't, the page states a maximum of $20,000 per dependent student per year, minus other aid. Before any loan money arrives: entrance counseling and a signed Master Promissory Note (both carded in the Receiving Aid department — not yet built).

“Up to $5,500 depending on grade level and dependency status”— the site’s own words, source page

“I thought grants were free — why would I owe one back?”

Generally they don't have to be repaid — the page's two triggers: you withdraw from school before finishing an enrollment period (a semester, say), or you receive a TEACH Grant and don't complete the service obligation. On an overpayment, you have 45 days to pay that portion back in full or enter a satisfactory repayment arrangement — if you do neither, you lose eligibility for further federal student aid.

“Most types of grants, unlike loans, are sources of financial aid that generally do not have to be repaid”— the site’s own words, source page

“What about the Iraq and Afghanistan Service Grant?”

Not listed on the grants page at all — its public content is a Help Center article: the award equals the maximum Pell Grant award for the year based on eligibility, can't exceed your cost of attendance, and carries an automatic percentage reduction (sequestration) depending on the date of the first disbursement. Its full eligibility rules were not read for this aid — the article governs.

“The Iraq and Afghanistan Service Grant award equals the maximum Pell Grant award for the year based on eligibility”— the site’s own words, source page

“Why was I told grad PLUS changed in 2026?”

The loans page states it plainly: graduate and professional students who do not qualify — or no longer qualify — for the limited exception on or after July 1, 2026 are not eligible to borrow Direct PLUS Loans. Callers near that line need the page (and their school), not a summary.

“Graduate and professional students who do not qualify (or no longer qualify) for the limited exception on or after July 1, 2026, are not eligible to borrow Direct PLUS Loans”— the site’s own words, source page

“Can I still get a Perkins Loan?”

No new ones: the authority for schools to make new Federal Perkins Loans ended on September 30, 2017. Existing Perkins Loans still exist and are serviced (by the school or its agency) — repayment and cancellation rules for them live in their own corners of this aid.

“The authority for schools to make new Federal Perkins Loans ended on Sept. 30, 2017”— the site’s own words, source page

“What is Federal Work-Study?”

Part-time jobs for undergraduate and graduate students with financial need, earning money toward education expenses. You'll earn at least the current federal minimum wage; your school must pay you at least once a month; the amount you earn can't exceed your total Work-Study award. The award itself depends on when you apply, your level of need, and your school's funding level — and the page's standing advice: funds are limited, so apply for aid early.

“Federal Work-Study provides part-time jobs for undergraduate and graduate students with financial need”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What am I really agreeing to when I take a loan?”

The page's own hard words, worth having at the desk: when you sign your promissory note, you're agreeing to repay according to its terms even if you don't complete your education, can't get a job after the program, or didn't like the education you received. You must make payments on time even if you never receive a bill or a reminder. And award amounts for subsidized, unsubsidized, and graduate PLUS loans are reduced for less-than-full-time enrollment.

“You are required to make payments on time even if you don't receive a bill, repayment notice, or a reminder”— the site’s own words, source page
