Public Service Loan Forgiveness

120 qualifying payments · Direct Loans · full-time public service

Official resources

  • PSLF Help Tool
  • PSLF Employer Search
  • The PSLF form (PDF)
  • PSLF Buyback
  • Submit a PSLF Reconsideration Request

PSLF form help line: 1-888-303-7818 (printed on the official form)

“What's the basic deal with PSLF?”

The remaining balance is forgiven after 120 qualifying monthly payments on Direct Loans, made under an accepted repayment plan while working full-time for an eligible employer.

  • It takes at least 10 years to get there.
“forgives the remaining balance on your Direct Loans after you’ve made the equivalent of 120 qualifying monthly payments”— the site’s own words, source page

“Do the 120 payments have to be in a row?”

No. Payments need not be consecutive — time with a non-qualifying employer pauses the count; it doesn't erase it.

“make a total of 120 qualifying monthly payments that need not be consecutive”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Is my employer eligible?”

Government at any level (federal, state, local, tribal — including U.S. military service), 501(c)(3) nonprofits, and certain other nonprofits that devote most of their full-time staff to qualifying public services. For-profits, labor unions, and partisan political organizations do NOT qualify. AmeriCorps and Peace Corps full-time service counts.

  • Check a specific employer in the PSLF Employer Search (linked above).
“Qualifying employment for PSLF isn’t about the specific job that you do for your employer—it’s about who you work for.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What counts as full-time?”

A weekly average of at least 30 hours for a qualifying employer — hours across qualifying employers can be combined. Teachers on contracts of at least 8 months are deemed full-time for the whole year; non-tenure-track higher-ed faculty multiply each credit/contact hour by at least 3.35. Paid vacation, paid leave, and FMLA leave count.

“a weekly average, alone or when combined, equal to at least 30 hours”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Which of my loans qualify?”

Direct Loans only — Subsidized, Unsubsidized, PLUS, and Direct Consolidation. FFEL and Perkins loans can become eligible by consolidating into a Direct Consolidation Loan. Private loans never qualify.

“Any loan received under the William D. Ford Federal Direct Loan (Direct Loan) Program qualifies for PSLF.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I consolidated — did I lose my payment count?”

For consolidations on or after September 1, 2024, earlier qualifying payments on the included Direct Loans are credited by weighted average. The page's own example: 60 payments on a $30,000 Direct Loan consolidated with a $30,000 loan with 0 payments becomes a new count of 30.

  • Standing tip from the page: certify ALL qualifying employment before consolidating, so the weighted average is applied correctly.
“credited to your consolidation loan using a weighted average of those payments”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What makes a payment ‘qualifying’?”

The full amount shown on the bill, paid on or before the due date, for a payment made after October 1, 2007, while working full-time for a qualifying employer and repaying under a qualifying plan.

“a full, on‑time monthly payment you make while working full time for a qualifying employer”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Which repayment plans count?”

The income-driven plans the page names today — Repayment Assistance Plan, IBR, ICR, PAYE — plus the 10-year Standard Repayment Plan. NOT qualifying: Graduated, Extended, the Standard plan for Direct Consolidation Loans, and the Tiered Standard Plan.

  • On the 10-year Standard Plan the loan is normally paid off by payment 120, leaving nothing to forgive — the IDR plans are where forgiveness usually happens.
“all income-driven repayment (IDR) plans … and the 10-year Standard Repayment Plan”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I'm on the Tiered Standard Plan.”

Payments under the Tiered Standard Plan do not qualify for PSLF — and the Tiered Standard Plan doesn't count toward TEPSLF either. This is one of the OBBBA-era changes the page now carries.

“Payments made under the Tiered Standard Plan do not qualify for PSLF.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I was in a deferment / forbearance — did those months count?”

Some do, most don't. Counting (while in an eligible plan other than RAP, with certified employment for the same months): cancer treatment deferment, economic hardship deferment, military service deferment, post-active-duty student deferment, AmeriCorps forbearance, National Guard Duty forbearance, DoD Student Loan Repayment Program forbearance, and certain emergency/military administrative forbearances. In-school status, grace periods, and other deferments/forbearances do NOT count — but a servicer can waive a deferment/forbearance so the borrower can make payments that count.

“you can’t make a qualifying monthly payment while your loans are in an in-school status, a grace period, a deferment, or a forbearance”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Why hasn't my payment count moved?”

The count is updated only when a new PSLF form documents a new period of qualifying employment. No new form, no new count. Certify every year and at every job change; after submitting, the borrower gets a letter with the updated count and can track it under My Loans on the dashboard.

“The number of qualifying payments you have made will be updated only when you submit another PSLF form”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How do I certify my employment / apply?”

One form does both jobs — submit it through the PSLF Help Tool. The tool checks the employer, prepares the form, and can request the employer's certification electronically (the employer gets a DocuSign email from dse_NA4@docusign.net). At 120 qualifying payments, the same form is the forgiveness application.

“To be considered for PSLF, you only need to submit a PSLF form. The easiest way to do this is by using the PSLF Help Tool.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can my employer type their signature?”

No. Typed or cursive-font signatures and certificate-based digital signatures are NOT accepted. Accepted: hand-drawn (finger, mouse, or pad), a scanned image of a hand-drawn signature, or a wet signature in its original format.

Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“My employer closed / won't sign.”

They can submit alternative documentation through the Help Tool instead: IRS Form W-2s, or an IRS-issued document (such as a tax transcript with the W-2 data) showing the FEIN/EIN, employer name, and wages for every calendar year in the employment period — or pay stubs for each month. Review takes additional time, and any month without supporting documentation can't be certified.

“any month that doesn’t have supporting documentation can’t be certified as qualifying employment”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I'm a contractor / paid through a PEO — do I count?”

A borrower must be a direct employee of the qualifying employer (one narrow exception exists where state law bars the employer from hiring directly, such as certain health-care settings). If a PEO handles payroll and benefits, the borrower is considered a direct employee of the non-PEO employer — certify with that employer.

Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I reached 120 — anything special on the final form?”

Yes: the borrower must still be working for a qualifying employer when they submit the form for forgiveness, and on the final form the employment period should be checked ‘still employed’ or certified (signed) by the employer in the same month as the employment end date.

“You must still be working for a qualifying employer at the time you submit your form for forgiveness.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Do Parent PLUS loans work with the Repayment Assistance Plan?”

Per the page as it stands today: Direct PLUS Loans for parents, and Direct Consolidation Loans that include a parent PLUS loan, are ineligible for the Repayment Assistance Plan and can't continue to qualify for PSLF through IBR or ICR as before. Handle with care — this is an OBBBA change still settling; walk the caller to the page itself.

“Direct PLUS Loans for parents and Direct Consolidation Loans that include a parent PLUS loan are ineligible for the Repayment Assistance Plan.”— the site’s own words, source page

“I disagree with my PSLF payment count — what can I do?”

Request a PSLF reconsideration — but only for this: you disagree with the qualifying payment count you received in a letter (from FSA or a prior PSLF servicer) or the count displayed on your StudentAid.gov account. It is NOT for progress updates — that's a new PSLF form through the Help Tool — and not for separating a Joint Consolidation Loan.

“You should only submit a reconsideration request, if you disagree with the qualifying payment count you received”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How do I submit a PSLF reconsideration request — step by step?”

Four steps, about five minutes total:

  • Step 1 — First review the program requirements for qualifying payments to confirm your situation warrants reconsideration. (The PSLF Help Tool can also check an employer and what actions you may need to take.)
  • Step 2 — Gather digital copies of anything that supports your case — payment-history documents, or letters you received from FSA or a prior PSLF servicer. Documentation is not required, but you can upload it as part of the request.
  • Step 3 — Click ‘Submit a reconsideration request’ on the reconsideration page and log in; you'll first be asked to review your borrower information.
  • Step 4 — Fill out the reconsideration request form. You can enter multiple periods in ONE request.
“Submitting multiple requests will delay the review process.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Is there a deadline for a reconsideration request?”

For a count received in a letter from the PSLF servicer, yes: if the letter is dated July 1, 2023 or after, the request must be submitted within 90 days of the date of the letter. (For letters dated before July 1, 2023, the deadline was December 29, 2023 — now past.) The page doesn't describe the review timeline or how the outcome is delivered for general reconsideration requests — the site doesn't say, so don't invent it.

“you must submit your PSLF reconsideration request within 90 days of the date of the letter”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What is PSLF Buyback?”

A way to buy back months that didn't count as qualifying payments because you were in an ineligible deferment or forbearance — by paying what you would have paid for those months. The gate: buyback is available ONLY if you already have 120 months of qualifying employment certified (PSLF or TEPSLF); the bought-back months then complete the 120 payments. The months must be on Direct Loans with a positive balance and coincide with approved qualifying employment. You can buy back months as far back as October 2007 (for consolidation loans, back to that loan's first disbursement date). Not having a qualifying job today is fine, as long as you were employed in the month of your 120th payment and the loan still has a positive balance.

“buy back months that don't count as qualifying payments”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Which months CAN'T be bought back?”

Months in these loan statuses: in-school or in-origination, in-grace, default, bankruptcy, or total-and-permanent-disability monitoring. Also excluded: months on loans that are not Direct Loans; months you were repaying under the Repayment Assistance Plan or the Tiered Standard Plan; months on paid-in-full, forgiven, or discharged loans; and months from loans included in a Direct Consolidation Loan or before that consolidation's first disbursement — buyback applies only to the current consolidation loan.

Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How much would I have to pay to buy back months?”

At least as much as you would have paid under an income-driven plan during those months — figured from your income and family size at that time, not your current income and family size. If the deferment or forbearance lasted less than a year, the lower of the IDR payments from just before or just after it is used; if the 10-year Standard amount is lower than the calculated IDR amount, the Standard amount is used. If you weren't on an IDR plan, they'll request that year's tax information — if it isn't sent within 30 days, the amount defaults to the 10-year Standard figure. Parent PLUS buyback amounts are figured on the 10-year Standard Plan. If the calculated amount is $0, forgiveness is processed when the agreement is sent — no payment required.

“at the time of the deferment or forbearance, not your current income and family size”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How do I request a buyback — step by step?”

Four steps:

  • Step 1 — Certify any unreported periods of qualifying employment first, using the PSLF Help Tool to submit a PSLF form.
  • Step 2 — Verify the deferment or forbearance months you want to buy back and confirm you have approved qualifying employment for those same months (in your StudentAid.gov account: My Loans → ‘view details’ → loan status history in the Loan Breakdown section; also confirm a positive balance).
  • Step 3 — Submit the request through PSLF Reconsideration: select ‘PSLF Buyback’ as the reconsideration type and confirm ‘Yes, I have 120 months of qualifying employment.’ An automated email confirms receipt — there is no way to check the status; the response comes by email.
  • Step 4 — If eligible, ED or your servicer sends a PSLF Buyback Agreement with the total amount and payment instructions. The servicer must receive the FULL amount within 90 days of the agreement date (multiple payments inside that window are allowed).
  • Keep making your regular payments until the buyback is approved. If the full amount isn't received within 90 days — or after the agreement you submit a PSLF form, apply or consolidate, pay the loan off, or the loan is discharged or forgiven — the agreement is void, and any amounts paid are credited as regular payments, not refunded.
“the full amount within 90 days from the date of your buyback agreement”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Reconsideration or buyback — which one do I need?”

Different tools for different problems. RECONSIDERATION: the count itself is wrong — payments or months were miscounted — and you want the determination re-reviewed. No money changes hands, and letter counts carry a 90-day deadline. BUYBACK: the count is right, but months in a deferment or forbearance didn't count, and you already have 120 months of qualifying employment — you pay what you would have paid in those months to make them count and reach forgiveness. The connection worth knowing: a buyback is requested THROUGH the PSLF Reconsideration door, choosing ‘PSLF Buyback’ as the reconsideration type.
