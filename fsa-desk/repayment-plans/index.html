Repayment Plans — the Fixed Plans

Standard · Tiered Standard · Graduated · Extended — and the July 1, 2026 dividing line

Official resources

  • Repayment Plans hub
  • Standard Repayment Plan
  • Graduated Repayment Plan

Plan changes run through your loan servicer · General help: 1-800-433-3243

“How do I pick a plan, or change the one I'm on?”

For the fixed plans (Standard, Tiered Standard, Graduated, Extended): contact your loan servicer. For an income-driven plan, the application lives in this aid's IDR area. You can also run the site's Repayment Calculator first for an early look at which plans you may be eligible for.

“If you want to be placed on one of these plans, contact your loan servicer”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I never picked a plan — what am I on?”

If you don't pick, your servicer places you: the Standard Plan for older loans. For Direct Loans disbursed on or after July 1, 2026, you are automatically enrolled in the Tiered Standard Plan when you leave school, unless you pick a different plan.

“If you don't pick a repayment plan, your loan servicer will place you on the Standard Plan”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“My loan is from after July 1, 2026 — which plans can I even get?”

Direct Loans disbursed on or after July 1, 2026 are NOT eligible for the Standard, Graduated, or Extended plans. On the IDR side: loans taken out before July 1, 2026 keep access to IBR, ICR, and PAYE — but a first disbursement on or after July 1, 2026 loses eligibility for those three, and eligible loans have access to the Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP) instead. The page also states the One Big Beautiful Bill Act eliminates ICR and PAYE entirely in the future (the IDR page dates that no later than July 1, 2028).

“Direct Loans disbursed on or after July 1, 2026, aren't eligible for the Standard Repayment Plan”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What is the Standard Plan?”

The basic plan: fixed payments for up to 10 years (10–30 years for consolidation loans), at least $50 a month. Payments can be higher than other plans, but total interest paid is usually lower and the payoff is usually sooner.

“The Standard Repayment Plan is the basic repayment plan”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What is the Tiered Standard Plan?”

THE SITE DOESN’T SAY — gap on the source page

The plan that replaces Standard for Direct Loans disbursed on or after July 1, 2026. The hub calls it a 10-year fixed-payment plan, and its plan table says loans on it are paid off within 10–25 years depending on the amount borrowed. GAP: the pages state no tier/amount schedule beyond that — the borrower's servicer holds their actual payment.

“replaces the Standard Repayment Plan for Direct Loans disbursed on or after July 1, 2026”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What is the Graduated Plan?”

Payments start lower and increase every two years, over up to 10 years (10–30 for consolidation loans). A payment is never less than the interest accruing between payments, and never more than three times any other payment.

“The Graduated Repayment Plan starts with lower payments that increase every two years”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What is the Extended Plan?”

Repayment stretched to up to 25 years, with fixed or graduated payments. Eligibility needs more than $30,000 in outstanding Direct Loans (a parallel rule applies to FFEL) and no Direct Loan balance as of October 7, 1998 (or on the date a later Direct Loan was obtained). The balance test applies per program — $35,000 of FFEL plus $10,000 of Direct qualifies the FFEL loans but not the Direct ones. Unavailable if you take a new Direct Loan disbursed on or after July 1, 2026.

“more than $30,000 in outstanding Direct Loans”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How long do I get to repay a consolidation loan on Standard or Graduated?”

It runs on the balance: under $7,500 → 10 years · $7,500–$9,999 → 12 · $10,000–$19,999 → 15 · $20,000–$39,999 → 20 · $40,000–$59,999 → 25 · $60,000 and up → 30. Other debt may be counted toward the bracket but can't exceed the consolidation loan's amount.

“Payments are fixed and made for up to 10 years (between 10 and 30 years for consolidation loans)”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Do these plans cover my Perkins Loan?”

No — Perkins Loan repayment plan options are not the same as Direct/FFEL plans. Check with the school for Perkins plan information.

“Perkins Loan repayment plan options are not the same as those for Direct Loan Program or FFEL Program loans”— the site’s own words, source page
