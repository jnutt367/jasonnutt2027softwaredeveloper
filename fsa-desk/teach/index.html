TEACH Grant — Managing the Obligation

Annual counseling, the Agreement to Serve or Repay, certifying each teaching year

Official resources

  • TEACH Grants

TEACH certification & conversion questions: 1-888-303-7818

“I'm getting a TEACH Grant this year — what must I do, in what order?”

Every year you receive a TEACH Grant, in this specific order, BEFORE that year's grant: ① complete TEACH Grant Initial and Subsequent Counseling, then ② sign an Agreement to Serve or Repay (Agreement) — both on the TEACH Grant page (StudentAid.gov/teach-grant-program) after logging in. You can check your counseling and Agreement completion by award year on the same page.

“you must complete, in this specific order: 1. TEACH Grant Initial and Subsequent Counseling, and then 2. An Agreement to Serve or Repay (Agreement)”— the site’s own words, source page

“How do I certify each teaching year — all my options?”

After you complete EACH of the four years, you must provide documentation of that teaching service; your chief administrative officer confirms you taught in a low-income school or ESA, met all state certification requirements, and that more than half your classes that year were in a high-need field. ★ ONLINE: submit and manage certification forms at StudentAid.gov/teach-certification. PAPER: mail or fax to Federal Student Aid Programs - TEACH, P.O. Box 300010, Greenville, TX 75403 · fax 540-212-2415. Certification questions: 1-888-303-7818.

“After you have completed each one of your four years of required teaching, you must provide documentation of that teaching service”— the site’s own words, source page

“What happens if I don't finish the obligation?”

ALL TEACH Grants you received convert to Direct Unsubsidized Loans, repaid in full with interest charged from the date of each TEACH Grant disbursement — not from the conversion date. You can also convert voluntarily at any time if you decide you won't fulfill the obligation (request at 1-888-303-7818); the page's own advice is that it's best to convert and repay as soon as possible to avoid accruing excessive interest.

“all TEACH Grants you received will be converted to Direct Unsubsidized Loans”— the site’s own words, source page

“What exactly is the TEACH service obligation?”

Serve as a full-time teacher for four elementary or secondary school years at a school or educational service agency (ESA) serving low-income students, teaching in a high-need field, meeting all state certification requirements for teaching — and complete the four years within eight years after you graduate or otherwise cease to be enrolled. One four-year obligation covers all TEACH Grants for undergraduate study, and a separate one covers all grants for graduate study. Note: the pages do NOT use the term “highly qualified” — the stated standard is meeting all state certification requirements.

“serve as a full-time teacher for four elementary or secondary school years at a school or educational service agency (ESA) that serves low-income students”— the site’s own words, source page

“Do I owe any counseling when I leave the school?”

Yes — TEACH Grant Exit Counseling: before you stop attending the school where you received your TEACH Grant, you must complete it. (Converted-loan counseling also exists on the program page: TEACH Grant Conversion Counseling.)

“Before you stop attending the school where you received your TEACH Grant, you must complete TEACH Grant Exit Counseling”— the site’s own words, source page

“Can the eight-year clock pause?”

Yes — suspension of the eight-year period, granted one year at a time, for the listed conditions ONLY: enrollment in a TEACH-eligible program, instruction toward state licensure, or an FMLA-qualifying condition (those three share a combined three-year cap); active-duty military service, a military spouse's deployment/PCS orders, or living in a federally declared major disaster area (three years each). Requests are uploaded on StudentAid.gov or filed via the Forms Library. The page's boundary: those are the only conditions under which a suspension may be granted.

“The conditions above are the only conditions under which you may receive a suspension of the eight-year period”— the site’s own words, source page

“My grant converted to a loan — can that be undone?”

Only under limited circumstances. You may request reconsideration of the conversion if you can show you were satisfying your service obligation, or that the grant was converted in error — documentation may be needed. If approved, the loan is changed back to a TEACH Grant. Requests ride the same line: 1-888-303-7818. The pages state no filing deadline for reconsideration.

“A TEACH Grant that has been converted to a loan can be changed back to a grant only under limited circumstances”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can the TEACH obligation itself be discharged?”

The obligation may be canceled (discharged) if you die or become totally and permanently disabled — the discharge itself lives in this aid's Discharge department (the TPD area carries the rules; the Death card sits in Discharge & Forgiveness — General). Don't confuse this with Teacher Loan Forgiveness, a different program for loans, also in the discharge department.

“Your TEACH Grant service obligation may be canceled (discharged) if you die or if you become totally and permanently disabled”— the site’s own words, source page

THE PAGES DON'T STATE: a deadline for submitting each annual certification form, or a deadline for requesting reconsideration after a conversion, or how a recipient is warned before conversion happens. The certification form itself is OMB No. 1845-0158 and the Agreement is OMB No. 1845-0083 if a caller is reading numbers off their paperwork.
