Total & Permanent Disability Discharge

Discharge for borrowers who are totally and permanently disabled

Official resources

  • TPD discharge — how to qualify and apply

TPD questions: 888-303-7818 (as printed on the page)

“What is a TPD discharge?”

A discharge of Direct Loan, FFEL, and Perkins loans — and of a TEACH Grant service obligation — for a borrower who is totally and permanently disabled. If approved, those loans no longer have to be repaid.

“you may qualify for a discharge of your federal student loans and/or Teacher Education Assistance for College and Higher Education (TEACH) Grant service obligation”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How do I prove I qualify?”

Three documentation routes: (1) qualifying VA documentation, (2) qualifying SSA documentation, or (3) certification by an authorized medical professional — who can certify electronically if the borrower provides their email address, or sign the completed application. Some borrowers are identified by SSA or VA and qualify automatically.

“Determine how you’ll qualify (VA, SSA, or medical professional)”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can my payments pause while I apply?”

Yes — payments can be paused for 120 days by submitting a TPD discharge application, or simply by contacting FSA and saying they plan to apply. Questions about loan eligibility: 888-303-7818.

“you can get your student loan payments paused for 120 days”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How do I submit the application?”

Apply online and upload the documents. Manual routes exist too: mail, fax, or the upload tool. An application plus the documentation showing they meet the requirements — both are required.

“Apply for TPD online and upload your documents.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What happens during the review?”

Loan payments are paused during the review. If a servicer is still asking for payment during that time, the borrower should contact the servicer.

“During the review process, loan payments are paused.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I was approved — do I get any payments back?”

The loans and/or TEACH Grant obligation are discharged, and the loan holder refunds payments received after a route-specific date: on/after the VA's disability determination date; after the date ED received the SSA documentation or certification; or after the date the medical professional certified the application.

“your loan holder will refund payments received”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What's the three-year monitoring period?”

A three-year monitoring period applies to discharges based on SSA or medical-professional certifications. If its conditions aren't met, the loan obligations may be reinstated. (The page's monitoring statement names SSA and medical certifications.)

“A three-year monitoring period applies for discharges based on SSA or medical certifications.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Who do I call with TPD questions?”

The page prints 888-303-7818 for questions about the eligibility of your loans.

“Contact us at 888-303-7818 if you have questions about the eligibility of your loans.”— the site’s own words, source page
