Total & Permanent Disability Discharge

Discharge for borrowers who are totally and permanently disabled

Official resources

  • TPD discharge — how to qualify and apply

TPD questions: 888-303-7818 (as printed on the page)

“How do I apply for TPD — and how do I send everything in? (all methods, one card)”

★ EASIEST — apply online (‘Apply for TPD Discharge’): complete the application online and upload your documents with it. A medical professional can certify electronically — give their email address and they sign through a DocuSign link; the site marks electronic signing ‘(Recommended)’. PAPER ROUTE: download the blank TPD Application, print it, complete and sign it (the medical professional signs the certification section on paper). Then a signed application — or any document for it — goes in through any of three doors: ① upload it after logging in to StudentAid.gov, at My Activity (the document upload tool) · ② mail to U.S. Department of Education, P.O. Box 300010, Greenville, TX 75403 · ③ fax to 540-212-2415. Already applied and something's missing? The same three doors — the pages give no separate missing-item process (see the missing-document card). There is no phone application: 888-303-7818 answers questions about loan eligibility only, and your loan servicer plays no part in applying.

  • An application plus the documentation for your route — both are required.
  • Signatures: hand-drawn or wet ink only — typed and certificate-based signatures are rejected (see the TPD signature card).
“Apply for TPD online and upload your documents.”— the site’s own words, source page

“What is a TPD discharge?”

A discharge of Direct Loan, FFEL, and Perkins loans — and of a TEACH Grant service obligation — for a borrower who is totally and permanently disabled. If approved, those loans no longer have to be repaid.

“you may qualify for a discharge of your federal student loans and/or Teacher Education Assistance for College and Higher Education (TEACH) Grant service obligation”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can my payments pause while I apply?”

Yes — payments can be paused for 120 days by submitting a TPD discharge application, or simply by contacting FSA and saying they plan to apply. Questions about loan eligibility: 888-303-7818.

“you can get your student loan payments paused for 120 days”— the site’s own words, source page

“I'm a veteran — what VA document proves I qualify?”

Documentation from the VA showing a VA disability determination on one of two grounds: you have one or more service-connected disabilities that are 100% disabling, OR you are totally disabled based on an individual unemployability rating. The document the page recommends: your Benefit Summary and Service Verification Letter. (VA Documentation page.)

“service-connected disabilities that are 100% disabling”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I receive Social Security disability — what SSA document proves I qualify?”

You must be eligible for SSDI or SSI and show one of these: your next continuing disability review has been scheduled within five to seven years from your last SSA disability determination · your next review has been scheduled at three years · your medical onset date for SSDI or SSI is at least five years before you apply · you qualify based on a compassionate allowance · or you now receive SSA retirement benefits and met one of the above right before qualifying for retirement. Proof: a copy of your SSA notice of award OR your Benefits Planning Query. Desk note: the pages use this review-schedule wording — there is no ‘medical improvement not expected’ category on them. (SSA Documentation page.)

“Your next continuing disability review has been scheduled within five to seven years”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Who can certify my TPD application — and what are they certifying?”

An authorized medical professional licensed to practice in the United States as one of: doctor of medicine (MD) · doctor of osteopathy/osteopathic medicine (DO) · nurse practitioner (NP) · physician assistant (PA) · certified psychologist at the independent practice level. They certify that you are unable to engage in any substantial gainful activity because of a physical or mental impairment that can be expected to result in death, has lasted a continuous period of at least 60 months, or can be expected to last at least 60 months. They provide their state and professional license number, which is verified for validity against state licensing databases — and they must answer every question in the certification, even if only to write ‘not applicable’: no blank boxes, no insurance codes or abbreviations. ED or its servicers may contact them if a question is left blank. Certification reaches FSA two ways: the professional completes it through the DocuSign email link triggered by the borrower's online application, or signs the paper application's certification section. The pages state no recency window for the certification.

“unable to engage in any substantial gainful activity”— the site’s own words, source page

“How do I prove I qualify?”

Three documentation routes: (1) qualifying VA documentation, (2) qualifying SSA documentation, or (3) certification by an authorized medical professional — who can certify electronically if the borrower provides their email address, or sign the completed application. Some borrowers are identified by SSA or VA and qualify automatically.

“Determine how you’ll qualify (VA, SSA, or medical professional)”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I got a letter saying I qualify for TPD automatically — do I need to do anything?”

No. FSA works with the VA and the SSA to identify people who qualify for TPD discharge. If one of those agencies tells FSA you're eligible, you're sent a letter — and you then get an automatic discharge unless you let them know you want to opt out. No application for this group.

“You'll then get an automatic discharge unless you let us know you want to opt out.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What happens during the review?”

Loan payments are paused during the review. If a servicer is still asking for payment during that time, the borrower should contact the servicer.

“During the review process, loan payments are paused.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How long does the TPD review take?”

THE SITE DOESN’T SAY — gap on the source page

The pages state no processing time, no review deadline, and no response-time promise — the site doesn't say, so don't quote one. What is promised: payments are paused while the application is being reviewed, and a borrower who is still receiving bills should contact their servicer.

“I was approved — do I get any payments back?”

The loans and/or TEACH Grant obligation are discharged, and the loan holder refunds payments received after a route-specific date: on/after the VA's disability determination date; after the date ED received the SSA documentation or certification; or after the date the medical professional certified the application.

“your loan holder will refund payments received”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What's the three-year monitoring period?”

A three-year monitoring period applies to discharges based on SSA or medical-professional certifications. If its conditions aren't met, the loan obligations may be reinstated. (The page's monitoring statement names SSA and medical certifications.)

“A three-year monitoring period applies for discharges based on SSA or medical certifications.”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“What can make my discharged loans come back during the monitoring period?”

One trigger, stated plainly: receiving a new student loan under the Direct Loan Program or a new TEACH Grant during the three-year monitoring period — the discharge is lost and the discharged loans and/or TEACH Grant service obligation are reinstated, and repayment resumes. Just as important at the desk: the current pages state NO income-reporting duty, NO earnings limit, and NO annual documentation requirement during monitoring — don't quote older rules. And a discharge based on VA documentation has no monitoring period at all.

“you'll lose your TPD discharge and have your discharged loans and/or TEACH Grant service obligation reinstated”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“I received a TPD discharge — can I ever borrow for school again?”

Yes, with two requirements: (1) give your school a letter from an authorized medical professional stating that you are once again able to engage in substantial gainful activity, and (2) sign a statement acknowledging that you can't get a TPD discharge of the new loan or TEACH Grant based on a disabling condition you already have, unless that condition significantly deteriorates in the future. The warning that belongs in the same breath: if they're still inside the three-year monitoring period, taking the new loan or grant reinstates the discharged loans (see the monitoring-trigger card).

“stating that you are once again able to engage in substantial gainful activity”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“My TPD application was denied — now what?”

The denial notice tells the borrower why they don't qualify, and their monthly payments (or TEACH Grant obligations) begin again. If they have NEW information that supports eligibility, they can request a reevaluation — within 12 months of the date they were notified the application was denied. After those 12 months, reevaluation is no longer available: they must submit a brand-new TPD discharge application. (From the ‘After You Apply’ page, linked from the TPD page.)

“you don't qualify for a TPD discharge, we will let you know why you were denied”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Is a TPD discharge taxable?”

Federal: no — for discharges received on or after January 1, 2018, the discharged amount is not treated as federal taxable income (before that date, it was). Which year it counts in depends on the route: VA-route borrowers are treated as receiving the discharge on the date it's approved; SSA and medical routes, at the END of the post-discharge monitoring period. If a Form 1099-C arrives, keep it for your records — it does not need to be included when filing the federal return. State: the discharged amount may be considered income for state tax purposes — the page directs borrowers to their state tax office or a tax professional before filing. (Taxes page.)

“may be considered income for state tax purposes”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Can someone else handle my TPD application for me?”

Yes. You can designate an individual or an organization — for example a family member, or an organization such as a veterans' service organization — to complete and submit your TPD discharge application on your behalf and to assist you throughout the process. You and your representative complete an Applicant Representative Designation form together, and the form must be processed before FSA can work with your representative. (The pages speak of designation; they say nothing about powers of attorney specifically.)

“complete and submit your Total and Permanent Disability (TPD) Discharge application on your behalf”— the site’s own words, source page
Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“How can the TPD application be signed — is a typed signature OK?”

The same signature law as PSLF. Accepted: a signature hand-drawn from a signature pad, mouse, or finger · a scanned photo of a signature hand-drawn on paper · a wet ink signature sent in its original format. NOT accepted: a signature typed in any font (including cursive fonts) · a certificate-based digital signature.

Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

“Does TPD cover a Parent PLUS loan — whose disability counts?”

THE SITE DOESN’T SAY — gap on the source page

The TPD pages never mention Parent PLUS, and never say whose disability counts for a PLUS loan. Their eligible-debt list names only Direct Loan Program loans, FFEL Program loans, and Perkins Loans. Flagged gap: don't build an answer on these pages — route the question by team practice.
