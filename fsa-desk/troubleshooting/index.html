Troubleshooting guide

Start from the symptom — what the caller is seeing — not from the program. Each entry: the symptom in the borrower’s words, the quick checks in order, what the site says quoted short and linked, and who owns the fix. Every entry is drawn from a card already on this desk and links back to it.

Built October 28, 2026 from this desk’s own cards, verified as their pages state. Two entries are honest gaps — where the site states no answer, the entry says so plainly and hands the caller to the official contact; nothing is invented to fill them.

Can’t log in — FSA ID trouble

“My account is locked.”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Stop guessing at the password — the lock came from failed attempts, and more guessing fixes nothing.
  2. Use one of the verification methods set up on the account: verified mobile phone or email, authenticator app, security key, or (if set up) challenge questions.
  3. Provide the month and day of your birth, continue, and enter the secure code you receive.
  4. Create a new password when prompted — that is the last step of unlocking.

What the site says:

“Your account is locked after three unsuccessful log-in attempts”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov Help Center — Help accessing your account

Who owns the fix: The borrower, at the log-in page. If no verification method is reachable, that is a human: FSA at 1-800-433-3243.

The desk card that carries it: FSA ID — account locked

“I forgot my password.”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Choose “Forgot Password?” on the log-in page.
  2. Provide your date of birth and verify your account.
  3. Set the new password — and don’t keep guessing in the meantime: multiple failed attempts can lock the account (the row above).

What the site says:

“Reset your password by providing your date of birth and verifying your account”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov Help Center — Help accessing your account

Who owns the fix: The borrower, at the log-in page. If the verification can’t be completed, FSA at 1-800-433-3243.

The desk card that carries it: FSA ID — forgot password

“I forgot my username — or which email I used.”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Choose “Forgot email, phone, or username?” on the log-in page.
  2. Provide your last name, date of birth, and SSN, then verify your account and receive a secure code.
  3. Shortcut worth knowing first: you can log in with your email address or mobile phone number instead of the username.

What the site says:

“Retrieve your email address, mobile phone number, or username by providing your personal information and verifying your account”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov Help Center — Help accessing your account

Who owns the fix: The borrower, at the log-in page. If the retrieval fails, FSA at 1-800-433-3243.

The desk card that carries it: FSA ID — forgot username

“I can’t get my two-step code — my phone is gone.”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Don’t create a new account — one person, one account.
  2. Use the backup code instead: it was generated when the account was created or when two-step verification was enabled.
  3. Enter the backup code where the two-step code is asked for.

What the site says:

“If you logged in but don’t have access to your two-step verification method, use your backup code”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov Help Center — Help accessing your account

Who owns the fix: The borrower, with the backup code. If the backup code is lost too, that is the lost-everything row below — a human at FSA, 1-800-433-3243.

The desk card that carries it: FSA ID — backup code

“It says my email is already used — can my parent and I share one?”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. No sharing: the email address must be unique to one person’s account — a parent’s or spouse’s account can’t use it either.
  2. Give the second person their own email address and their own account; each contributor signs with their own.
  3. Tip from the card: the account email also carries two-step verification codes — use an inbox the person actually reads.

What the site says:

“The email address you provide must be unique and can’t be used with anyone else’s StudentAid.gov account”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov — Creating Your Account

Who owns the fix: The borrower (and the parent), each with their own email and account.

The desk card that carries it: FSA ID — one email per account

“My account verification just says ‘Pending.’”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Check the clock before anything else: Social Security Administration verification usually happens right away, but a “Pending” status may take one to three days to resolve.
  2. There is nothing to re-submit while it pends — the wait is the step.

What the site says: The create-account page states that SSA verification usually happens right away and that “Pending” may take one to three days to resolve. It names no way to speed it up and no escalation for a longer wait.

Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

Who owns the fix: Nobody to call while it pends — it resolves on its own. Past three days, the desk’s answer is a human: FSA at 1-800-433-3243.

The desk card that carries it: FSA ID — create your account

“I lost EVERYTHING — my email, my phone, and my backup code.”

THE SITE DOESN’T SAY — honest gap, stated plainly

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Confirm they truly have none of the three: no access to the account email, no phone for two-step codes, no backup code written down.
  2. If any one of the three still works, use the rows above instead — they are faster than a call.

What the site says: THE SITE DOESN’T SAY: the pages publish no recovery path for a caller who has lost their email, their phone, AND their backup code — and no password-complexity rules either. Do not improvise account-recovery steps the site doesn’t publish.

Who owns the fix: A human at FSA: 1-800-433-3243. That is the whole answer this row has, on purpose.

The site itself — errors and outages

“The site won’t let me log in — is it down?” (on a Sunday morning)

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Check the clock first: regularly scheduled maintenance runs Sundays from 3 a.m. to 11 a.m. Eastern time.
  2. During that window you can view website pages, but you cannot create an account or log in.
  3. If it is inside the window, the fix is the end of the window — try again after 11 a.m. ET.

What the site says:

“StudentAid.gov has regularly scheduled site maintenance on Sundays from 3 a.m. until 11 a.m. Eastern time”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov Help Center — Unknown error

Who owns the fix: Nobody — it is scheduled, not broken. Outside the window, an unplanned problem goes to the Help Center and FSA at 1-800-433-3243.

The desk card that carries it: Outages — Sunday maintenance

“I got an unknown error message.”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Clear your browser history (your “cache”).
  2. Close all browser windows.
  3. Reopen your browser.
  4. Try again — in that order; it is the site’s own playbook.

What the site says:

“Clear your browser history (your “cache”)”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov Help Center — Unknown error

Who owns the fix: The borrower, in their browser. If the error survives the four steps, the page’s stated next step is the Federal Student Aid Information Center: 1-800-433-3243.

The desk card that carries it: Outages — unknown error

“Is my browser the problem?”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Use the latest release of a supported browser: Microsoft Edge, Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, or Apple Safari.
  2. Cookies must be enabled, with 128-bit encryption.
  3. PDFs need the free Adobe Acrobat Reader.

What the site says:

“Supported browsers: The latest release for Microsoft Edge, Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, and Apple Safari”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov — Site requirements

Who owns the fix: The borrower, in their browser. On an unsupported browser the site warns that viewing and interacting may be significantly impaired.

The desk card that carries it: Outages — supported browsers

“Where can I check whether the site is down?”

THE SITE DOESN’T SAY — honest gap, stated plainly

Quick checks, in order:

  1. There is no page to check: no public real-time system-status page exists on StudentAid.gov (searched for this desk, October 22, 2026).
  2. The Announcements page carries program updates, webinars, and events — not system status.
  3. There is no consolidated error-code list either — only individual error-message articles.

What the site says: THE SITE DOESN’T SAY: no channel for unplanned-outage notifications is published. During an unplanned problem, the stated channel is the Help Center and the 1-800 line — not a status page that doesn’t exist.

Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

Who owns the fix: FSA, through the Help Center and 1-800-433-3243.

The desk card that carries it: Outages — no status page · Outages — what the pages don’t say

“My payment didn’t post”

“I paid — but who did I actually pay?”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Payments go to the loan servicer, not to FSA — a payment sent anywhere else hasn’t reached the loan.
  2. Find the servicer: log in and visit My Aid / the account dashboard and choose “View loan servicer details.”
  3. The billing statement states the amount due; match the payment against it.

What the site says:

“Find your servicer by logging in below and visiting your My Aid page”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov — Loan servicers

Who owns the fix: The loan servicer — they hold the payment and the balance. (For a defaulted loan, the Default Resolution Group: 1-800-621-3115.)

The desk card that carries it: Balance & Payment — who you pay

“My auto debit didn’t come out — how does auto debit work?”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Auto debit is set up through the loan servicer, not on StudentAid.gov — questions about a scheduled debit start there.
  2. The benefit, so the caller knows it is set up right: an automatic monthly electronic debit from a checking or savings account, with a 0.25% interest-rate reduction on Direct Loans (a temporary 1% reduction runs July 1, 2026 – June 30, 2028 for qualifying Direct Loans; enrollment by December 31, 2026).

What the site says:

“you receive a 0.25% interest rate reduction on Direct Loans”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov — How Repayment Works

Who owns the fix: The loan servicer — they run the debit.

The desk card that carries it: Balance & Payment — auto debit

“I paid days ago — why doesn’t it show yet?”

THE SITE DOESN’T SAY — honest gap, stated plainly

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Confirm the payment went to the servicer (the row above) — FSA can’t trace a payment it never holds.
  2. Ask the servicer, who owns the payment, where it stands.

What the site says: THE SITE DOESN’T SAY: the pages state no posting timeframe — no number of days a payment takes to show — and no on-site balance view beyond the billing statement and the servicer. This row will gain a site’s-words line the day a public page states one; until then, no timeframe is invented here.

Who owns the fix: The loan servicer — the payment is theirs to trace.

Forms and applications

“Exit Counseling kicked me out — my session is gone.”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Know the rule before restarting: exit counseling must be finished IN ONE SESSION — an incomplete session cannot be saved and returned to.
  2. Sessions time out after 15 minutes of inactivity, so set aside the full sitting: about 30 minutes.
  3. Have ready before logging in: your verified account, your school name(s), and updated contact information.

What the site says:

“Exit counseling takes about 30 minutes to complete”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov — Loan Exit Counseling

Who owns the fix: The borrower restarts the session. Help completing it: the school’s financial aid office (a school may have alternate requirements), or FSA at 1-800-433-3243.

The desk card that carries it: Exit Counseling — complete it

“I submitted my FAFSA — did it go through?”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Log in to your StudentAid.gov account.
  2. Open “My Activity” and select the submission — its status stands there.
  3. Paper filers: the status can be checked roughly 7–10 days after mailing the form.

What the site says:

“Check the status of your form by logging in to your StudentAid.gov account”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov — Review and Correct Your FAFSA Form

Who owns the fix: The borrower, in My Activity. Form and process questions: FSA at 1-800-433-3243.

The desk card that carries it: FAFSA Corrections — check the status

“Something’s missing from my TPD application — where does it go?”

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Upload it through the same doors as the application: log in to StudentAid.gov and go to My Activity (the document upload tool).
  2. Or mail it: U.S. Department of Education, P.O. Box 300010, Greenville, TX 75403 · or fax: 540-212-2415.
  3. Honest flag from the card: the TPD pages give no missing-item-specific instruction — no separate notice or deadline is described.

What the site says:

“You may upload your application by logging in to StudentAid.gov and going to My Activity.”— the site’s own words, StudentAid.gov — Total and Permanent Disability Discharge

Who owns the fix: The borrower sends the document; questions about TPD go to discharge & forgiveness support: 1-888-303-7818.

The desk card that carries it: TPD — a missing document

“My upload won’t take — is it the file?”

THE SITE DOESN’T SAY — honest gap, stated plainly

Quick checks, in order:

  1. Try the doors that exist: the My Activity document upload tool after logging in; failing that, the mail and fax routes on the TPD row above always take paper.
  2. Don’t quote a file-size or file-type limit at the caller — there isn’t a published one to quote.

What the site says: THE SITE DOESN’T SAY: no page this desk has read states upload file-type or size limits, or remedies when an upload fails. The row stays a gap until a public page states them — limits are never invented here.

Open the source on StudentAid.gov ↗

Who owns the fix: For a TPD document: discharge & forgiveness support, 1-888-303-7818. Generally: FSA at 1-800-433-3243.

The desk card that carries it: TPD — a missing document
